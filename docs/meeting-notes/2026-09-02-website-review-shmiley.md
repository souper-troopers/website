## Decisions made

### 1. Shmiley stays in the background. No separate Shmiley website.
The strongest statement of the reasoning came from Adrian: Shmiley is the vehicle that lets Kerry
pay people, trade, and keep the admin and finances separate — but *"it's actually the Souper Troopers
shop. Shmiley is of no significance to the supporter of Souper Troopers."* Brad put the commercial
case: *"fundamentally, it's easier to build one brand than two"*, and a shopper buying Souper
Troopers Coffee doesn't know or care what Shmiley is.

Shan argued the other side — that Shmiley reads as a brand in its own right, deserves its own
identity, and *"is more than the African worry dolls and the coffee."* Kerry's position landed
between the two: Shmiley's purpose is income generation, reducing donor reliance, and employing and
upskilling previously homeless people; it is becoming *"more evident that it is Souper Troopers'
social enterprise"* rather than a standalone brand.

Hilton closed it: hold the name, don't push it, revisit as the business grows. Adrian: *"keep it in
the background, it can always grow."*

**Consequences:**
- The shop stays inside the Souper Troopers site. Products are branded **Souper Troopers** —
  Souper Troopers Coffee, the African Worry Dolls.
- **`shmiley.org` is kept and kept paid for.** Kerry confirmed the domain already redirects to the
  Souper Troopers page — *"we realised yesterday it does actually, if you click on it, go to the
  Souper Troopers page."* Nobody is to let it lapse.
- Stephen noted a second Shmiley site remains cheap to add later — one product inventory can publish
  to two domains — so nothing about this decision is hard to reverse. Deprioritised, not closed.
- There is no Shmiley Instagram or other consumer-facing Shmiley presence, which Brad took as
  confirmation there is no audience for a Shmiley site to serve.

### 2. The homepage gets cut back to a landing page.
Adrian's point, agreed by everyone: *"the homepage is just a landing page. You say wow, and then you
go across your bar… only on the homepage is crucial stuff."* Brad wanted it reduced to roughly three
statements. Adrian: *"people don't like to read."*

Stephen agreed the page currently repeats itself (the stats block especially) and said it can be
slimmed substantially. The mechanism agreed: once the hero video exists it fills the vertical space
on landing, everything else moves below the fold.

This was where the point was first made, but it did not stay about the homepage — see decision 3.

### 3. Less copy, across the whole site — not just the homepage
This ran through the entire walkthrough rather than belonging to one page, and it was Adrian's main
theme. On the homepage: *"there's all this wording, all this wording… I think the message should
come through of, as Brad has said, three fundamental pillars of what Souper Troopers is about.
**That's my keep it simple, stupid** — that's my key to this."* And a minute later, plainly:
*"people don't like to read, guys."*

The same note was struck on every page after it. Brad on Get Involved and donate: *"there's so much…
it's overwhelming almost, at this stage, how much."* Stephen agreed rather than defended, and said so
repeatedly — the stats block *"I'm repeating a little bit too often"*, the donate page *"a bit of a
mess at the moment"*, Get Involved *"a bit information dense."* His own summary: *"we don't want to
fire-hose these people with information, so we have to be judicious in what we include"*, and
**less is more should always be a lever we can pull**.

**Four ways to take copy out came up, and they aren't the same thing:**
- **Cut the repetition first.** The stats block and the calls to action repeat across several pages.
  This is the cheapest reduction because nothing is actually lost — Stephen: *"there is some
  repetition on this page, so it won't be a problem to slim it down substantially."*
- **Hide detail rather than delete it.** Adrian's drop-down proposal for Get Involved is this: the
  headline choices visible, the detail underneath, expanding when asked for — *"it'll be cleaner and
  better."* The words survive; the wall of them doesn't.
- **Replace copy with video.** Brad on the success stories: a short clip *"instead of the copy."*
- **Replace copy with images.** Shan suggested a gallery treatment with text over pictures, and Brad
  confirmed the image-led version read better on screen.

⚠ **Brad and Adrian were not asking for the same thing, and it's worth not blurring them.** Adrian's
position is that there is too much text and it should go. Brad's was explicitly the opposite of
deletion — *"I don't know if you need to remove. I'm not saying remove"* — his fix is sequencing:
capture someone at the top, and then *"you read all this amazing information"*, because *"anyone
that's interested is going to get to it."* The drop-downs and the hero video satisfy both readings
at once, which is why they were the parts everyone agreed on quickest.

**What Stephen needs back:** *"it's not always easy to tell what I can remove… if you guys feel that
stuff can just be sliced out, then by all means."* Cutting repetition is ours to do. Deciding which
of Kerry's actual words go is not — that needs someone to say so.

### 4. The homepage hero becomes a short video, and Brad is producing it.
Replaces the current Mandela mural splash. Brad's concept, which he undertook to write up and send
round: roughly **30 seconds, shot at the Hub, with Kerry and someone who has been through the
programme** — *"you're captured, and then you read everything on the homepage."*

Shan is already shooting: content was shot two weeks ago, and the Hub is being physically rearranged
because the current space is too dark to film well — *"between tomorrow and Friday we're going to be
shooting content."*

Stephen's constraint on the call: the site is deliberately built to load almost instantly and a
large video works against that. His preference if there is a trade-off: **shorter and polished over
longer and grainy.** Kerry: *"if it's too long, trust and believe we can pull heartstrings in 10
seconds."*

The full technical brief — length, format, and what to avoid on the day — is on the website's own
update page, with the rest of the video discussion, so it stays in one place rather than spread
across email threads. **One point matters before anyone shoots:** a video with talking in it cannot
start on its own, because browsers block sound from playing automatically. So the plan needs a short
silent clip for behind the headline *as well as* Brad's story piece, which sits below it and plays
when someone clicks.

### 5. Success stories get short video clips, not more copy.
Brad: a 30-second clip per person — he named Peaceful, Gideon, Umar — *"instead of the copy… a video
is huge."* Stephen confirmed this is straightforward: they sit below the fold so they can be lazily
loaded and won't slow the page. The team supplies the videos.

### 6. "Get Involved" is restructured by audience, not by donation type.
Adrian's proposal, which the group liked immediately: lead with
**personal vs corporate**, as expanding drop-downs with image banners and titles, and put the detail
underneath. Kerry: *"what you can donate as an individual or a company… you speak differently
according to the audience."* Stephen to come back with a design.

This doubles as the clearest example of decision 3: the drop-downs mean the page can carry the same
detail while showing far less of it at once — *"it'll be cleaner and better."*

### 7. Zapper comes off the donate page.
Kerry is *"99.9% positive"* Souper Troopers no longer uses Zapper — they moved to SnapScan. It was
inherited from the old site. To be removed.

### 8. Partners: add links, and Woolworths goes to the top.
- Each partner logo should link to that partner's own site (currently none do).
- Space to be made for **Woolworths**, who Kerry expects to become the biggest partner yet and who
  are about to sell the dolls.
- On ordering, the group moved off strict alphabetical. Stephen raised the open-source
  gold/silver-sponsor pattern and the group settled on the softer version: **Woolworths at the top
  with a larger banner**, without formally tiering everyone else. Brad's reasoning: the point of the
  section is to show *"credible brands backing everything."*
- Kerry flagged that the current partner list is **outdated** and needs revising.
- Explicitly carried to the next meeting: *"how can we get Woolworths' banner massive."*

### 9. Navigation: "Donate" becomes a Support menu with Donate and Shop under it.
Adrian's fix for the disconnect he spotted — the homepage says nothing about the shop, then the shop
page opens with Shmiley branding. A single contrast-coloured nav item that drops down to **Shop** or
**Donate** makes the shop discoverable from the front door and gives the visitor the two things they
can actually do. Stephen: *"that's a pretty cool frame."*

### 10. The shop page must state plainly where the money goes.
Brad: make it *"as bold as you can"* at the top of the shop — that proceeds from these sales go back
into the work. This is what replaces explaining Shmiley to the visitor.

### 11. Shop product photography is being reshot.
Kerry on the current product images: *"100% going to change."* Shan is shooting them the following
day. Stephen's only requirement: consistent quality and resolution across the set.

## Not decided — carried forward

### The big one: should the site lead with "the Humanity Hub"?
Opened by Shan, and it is a discoverability finding rather than a branding preference: research and
AI-driven scans surface Souper Troopers as **a food scheme** rather than as the service centre it
actually is. The name's origin — Kerry serving soup from her car boot — is now actively misleading
about what the organisation does.

- Shan floated inverting it: make the site *the Humanity Hub*, with Souper Troopers as the origin
  story.
- Adrian: the physical address genuinely is Souper Troopers / the Humanity Hub, *"because everything
  happens inside the building"* — including production of the products. He proposed **"The Humanity
  Hub by Souper Troopers."**
- Hilton: *"the Humanity Hub is probably a name that's easier to play with and attract to."* He drew
  the Marks & Spencer parallel — a business that began as a penny bazaar and rebranded a decade on —
  and said this needs proper strategy, not a website tweak.
- Kerry, as founder, was open to it: *"I've always been open to detaching from the name when it makes
  sense… 'the Humanity Hub by Souper Troopers' — things are just starting to fall into place."* She
  noted the counter-pressure: people on the streets know the name Souper Troopers, and there's a
  community built around it.

**Status: strategy question, not a website question.** Hilton's condition on any change:
*"there's going to be no changes made unless there's unanimous excitement about it."* Kerry to brief
her colleagues and board. Brad had to leave before this discussion and will be filled in, and will
discuss it with Kerry the following week.

### Less copy versus being found — not raised on the call
Flagging this because it *wasn't* discussed, and it should be before anything gets cut. A good deal
of work over August went into making the site findable, both by Google and by AI assistants, which
is increasingly how people search. Both work by reading the words on a page. Less text means less to
match against, so "cut the copy" and "be found" pull against each other.

This is not an argument against decision 3 — the site does carry too much, and Adrian is right about
that. But the reductions differ in what they cost:

- **Cutting repetition costs nothing.** The same stats block appearing on five pages helps nobody,
  search engines included.
- **Hiding detail behind drop-downs costs nothing either.** The words stay in the page and are still
  read; they just aren't all shown at once.
- **Deleting text outright is the one with a real cost**, and it lands hardest on the pages written
  to answer the questions people actually type.

So the sensible order is the cheap reductions first. There's a fair chance the site feels right long
before anything has to be traded away. Worth ten minutes at the next meeting rather than a decision
now.

### Fonts — deliberately left open
Kerry raised that the brand fonts are **Montserrat and Patrick Hand**, and that the old website used
neither. Stephen has currently reverted the site to a system font, for a specific reason: he doesn't
want the page to visibly jump and reflow as a custom font loads. He'll look at whether the brand
fonts can be used without that cost.

Neither side pushed. Kerry: *"I can't say I'm attached — only whether either of those fonts bring
more personality to the pages."* Stephen: *"it's a single line of code, we can change it any time, so
we don't necessarily want to make the decision up front."*

### The CAST model needs a proper infographic
Kerry wants it *"more playful"* and wants the *feeling* of the programme to come through —
*"Souper Troopers is a feeling."* Stephen was straightforward that design isn't his strength and
asked for a designer; **Brad offered a designer friend.** Stephen's only constraint: it has to sit
coherently with the rest of the site.

Brad asked whether a single graphic hurts SEO. Stephen: only if it contains text that needs
indexing — and text inside a graphic can be vectorised into real copy if needed.

### Smaller items raised and left open
- **Sticky/auto-updating nav** as you scroll past sections (Brad asked; Stephen: possible, but not
  planned for this build).
- The **"Get Involved" and donate pages both look thin** — Stephen's own view, partly down to a
  shortage of media to work with. Shan suggested a gallery treatment with text over images; Brad
  confirmed the image-led version looked better on screen.
- **Stats and figures on the site are not yet accurate** (Kerry) and need real numbers.
- Stephen flagged that **the same stats block repeats too often** across pages.
