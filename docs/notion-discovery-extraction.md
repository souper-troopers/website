### What's wrong with the current site (drives redesign priorities)
- Doesn't tell visitors what to do first — why Souper Troopers is different, what a donation achieves, what action to take.

- Doesn't feel inviting — needs more people-in-action imagery: workshops, counselling, coffee, laughter, doll-making, shared meals, visits.
- Impact numbers should be unmissable: people supported, employment placements, counselling sessions, skills workshops, volunteers.
- Success stories should outrank organisational info — people are the biggest asset.
- If changing only one thing: rebuild the homepage around **"How does Souper Troopers change lives?"** — feel the impact, don't just explain the org.

### CAST Programme
- Ethos: **Dignity. Love. Respect.** Every beneficiary is seen as human first, before being homeless.
- The CAST journey:
  - **C — Connect**: beneficiary requests assistance, appointment scheduled with a social worker; this first appointment starts accountability.
  - **A — Assess**: social worker and individual jointly assess the situation, identify basic needs, map the individual journey — rooted in dignity, love, respect.
  - **S — Support**: tailored, holistic support — rehabilitation programs, reunification services, ID document assistance, skills upliftment (short training courses), employment/job-readiness support (CV writing, interview prep), referrals to shelters/clinics/other resources.
  - **T — Transform**: with support in place, the person moves toward independence and becomes a success story. Every journey is unique; CAST is the shared framework.
  - Likely to become a major homepage section.
- Success stories named so far: **Peaceful, Odette, Gideon** — job titles known but full personal narratives, quotes, photos, and consent still outstanding.
  - From the live team page (2026-08-08): **Odette Oliver** is now the African Worry Doll supervisor; **Gideon Venter** is admin and fieldwork assistant — both current staff, not just past beneficiaries. Strong, concrete transformation detail even without full narratives yet.
- Also referenced as underused stories: **Umar** (became a personal trainer), **Sidney** (rebuilding his life), **African Worry Dolls** (creating employment).
