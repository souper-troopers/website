# Reference Site Design Principles for Souper Troopers

## Sites reviewed
- Social Bite — strong mission-driven storytelling, clear visitor paths, vibrant impact messaging
- Orange Sky — simple service-first messaging, clear navigation for people in need and supporters
- U-Turn Homeless Ministries — journey-based structure, credible partner/trust signals, strong donation focus
- OzHarvest and Homeless Hub were noted as favorites but blocked from automated scraping; their influence is still included in the thematic summary.

## Shared design principles

### 1. Bold, human-first messaging
- Strong opening line that clearly states the problem and the human connection.
- Use phrases like “We are all human,” “Together we are the change,” “Free laundry, showers and genuine kōrero,” and “Let’s end homelessness, together.”
- The homepage should immediately convey both empathy and action.

### 2. Clear audience pathways
- Explicit visitor journeys for different supporters: donate, volunteer, partner, shop.
- Visual cards or sections that let people choose their path quickly.
- Keep the homepage from feeling like a brochure; it should serve as a gateway to specific actions.

### 3. Impact metrics and proof points
- Show measurable results near the top: number of people helped, donations delivered, partnerships, location reach.
- Use simple, memorable numbers with short explanatory text.
- Proof can be reinforced with logos, quotes, or a brief story.

### 4. Program or journey storytelling
- Explain the program flow in a few steps: who is helped, how they are supported, and what the outcome is.
- Use language like “CAST journey,” “Humanity Hub,” or “participant pathway” so the visitor understands the mechanism.
- Avoid long paragraphs; prefer concise bulleted or numbered content.

### 5. Strong donate/shop CTA placement
- Make the primary support action obvious in the hero and again in the mid-page flow.
- Support both direct giving and commerce-based support (shop, buy a product).
- Use contrasting buttons and clear labels like “Donate now” and “Shop gifts + coffee.”

### 6. Emotional storytelling with simple visuals
- Human stories, quotes, or testimonials reinforce the mission.
- Keep the design warm and personal rather than overly corporate.
- A single strong story or image on the homepage is often more effective than many weak visuals.

### 7. Trusted partner and supporter signals
- Show logos or names of partners, supporters, and credibility markers (NPO, PBO, B-BBEE if relevant).
- This is particularly important for fundraising and business partnerships.

### 8. Minimal top-level navigation
- Keep primary nav options to 5–7 items, such as Home, About, Our Work, Shop, Donate, Get Involved, Contact.
- Avoid too many menu items that dilute the focus.

## Practical lessons for Souper Troopers

### Homepage focus
- Hero: mission + primary action
- Paths: support, partner, shop
- Proof: impact + story
- Program: CAST / Humanity Hub explained simply
- CTA: donate, shop, contact

### Secondary pages
- About: mission, story, partners
- Our Work: programs, participant journey, impact
- Shop: products with purpose and support explanation
- Get Involved: donate, volunteer, donate goods, corporate support
- Donate: payment methods, why now, impact examples
- News/Updates: momentum and social proof

## How this expands the wireframe
- The homepage should be more structured around the audience paths and impact than just story copy.
- Add a compact program journey section that explains how support becomes real outcomes.
- Add a small partner/support logos section to build trust.
- Keep the shop and donate paths clearly separate but connected by the mission.

## Recommended next page concepts
1. **About / Why we exist** — mission, story, values, partners.
2. **Our Work** — the CAST/Humanity Hub journey and outcomes.
3. **Get Involved** — specific supporter actions for individuals and businesses.
4. **Shop** — products, purpose, support benefits.
5. **Donate** — direct donation options and urgency.

## Notes

- U-Turn: clear journey and trust-focused messaging.
